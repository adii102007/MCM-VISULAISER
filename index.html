Understand what DP is actually computing for matrix chain multiplication

The matrix chain
You are given a chain of matrices. Their sizes come from the shared dimension array p. Matrix Ai has size pi−1 × pi.
What we are choosing
Multiplying matrices is associative: (AB)C = A(BC). But the number of scalar multiplications depends on where we put the parentheses. Finding the cheapest parenthesization is the entire problem.
Why we cannot brute-force
The number of ways to fully parenthesize a chain of n matrices is the (n−1)-th Catalan number. That grows exponentially:
The DP recurrence
Let M[i, j] be the minimum cost of multiplying Ai…Aj. Then:
M[i, j] = min over i ≤ k < j of M[i, k] + M[k+1, j] + pi−1 · pk · pj
M[i, i] = 0
What each term means
M[i, k] Minimum cost to multiply the left part Ai…Ak.
M[k+1, j] Minimum cost to multiply the right part Ak+1…Aj.
pi−1·pk·pj Cost of merging the two resulting matrices, whose sizes are pi−1 × pk and pk × pj.
Overlapping subproblems
A subchain like M[2, 3] appears inside many larger subproblems (M[1, 3], M[2, 4], M[1, 4]…). Naive recursion recomputes it every time. DP computes it once and stores it. That is the entire reason DP exists here.

The DP table M[i, j]
Cells with i = j are 0 (single matrix). Cells above the diagonal are filled by increasing chain length.
Order of computation
Bottom-up builds the table by chain length: length 2, then 3, then 4, up to length n. That is why every M[i, k] and M[k+1, j] is already known when we compute M[i, j].
